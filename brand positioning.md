## 01 · Executive Summary

Poivre Paris is a Paris-rooted home and tableware brand built for a generation that wants their home to feel curated, calm, and quietly expensive — without the price tag, gatekeeping, or stuffiness of traditional luxury. We sit at an underserved intersection of the market: quiet-luxury aesthetics, sculptural design, accessible pricing, and a digitally native, trend-aware brand voice.

Where mass-market players (IKEA, H&M Home, Zara Home) compete on volume and price, and design-led labels (Ferm Living, HAY, B&B Italia) compete on craft at a premium, Poivre claims the white space between them: design-led objects that feel like quiet luxury, priced for the apartment-dweller, marketed for the feed.

> *"Objects that make your home feel expensive."*

### The Positioning Statement

For style-aware women aged 22–40 building a home that reflects who they are, Poivre Paris is the home and tableware brand that delivers quiet-luxury design as objects you can actually afford — combining the sculptural restraint of high-end European interiors with the accessibility of a digitally native, drop-based brand. Unlike fast-furniture giants or aspirational labels priced out of reach, Poivre treats every object as a small piece of art for everyday rituals.

### The One-Liner

**Poivre Paris — quiet luxury for the way you live now.**

## 02 · Market & Opportunity

### Category Dynamics

The home décor and tableware category is large, durable, and structurally favorable for a brand like Poivre:

- **Fragmented.** No single dominant brand owns the design-led mid-market — the field is wide open.
- **Branding-heavy and highly visual.** Buyers choose on aesthetic and identity more than spec — branding is the moat.
- **Margin-friendly.** Ceramics, glass, and small home objects support healthy unit economics.
- **Ecommerce + social media driven.** TikTok, Reels, and Pinterest are now the primary discovery layer for home goods.
- **Steady growth.** Global home décor: ~5% CAGR. Premium tableware/ceramics: 5–7% CAGR.

### Where the Growth Is

The strongest growth is not in mass-market basics. It is in the segment Poivre is built for:

- Premium-looking mid-market brands
- Artisanal and handmade-looking aesthetics
- Curated, edited collections rather than endless catalogs
- Quiet luxury — restraint, materiality, no logos
- Natural materials: stone, wood, ceramic, glass, linen
- Design-led tableware as a daily ritual category

### The Cultural Tailwind

Three forces are working in our favor. First, the post-pandemic re-centering of the home as the stage for identity, hosting, and self-care. Second, the maturation of "quiet luxury" from a fashion micro-trend into a durable interior aesthetic. Third, a generational shift — Gen Z and younger Millennials buy aesthetics first and brand-name luxury second, which makes our value proposition unusually well-timed.

## 03 · Competitive Landscape

### The Perceptual Map

We map the category on two axes: **Accessible ↔ Aspirational Luxury**, and **Functional ↔ Artistic/Sculptural**.

**Functional + Accessible**
IKEA, H&M Home, MUJI, Leroy Merlin. Mass-market basics. Volume game. Not our fight.

**Functional + Aspirational**
Crate & Barrel, Williams Sonoma, Zara Home. Polished but conventional. Aspirational but rarely artistic.

**Sculptural + Aspirational**
Ferm Living, B&B Italia, Hay (upper end). Design-led, beautifully made, but priced out of reach for our core customer.

**Sculptural + Accessible — Our Quadrant**
This is where the white space lives. Anthropologie and Maisons du Monde lean here but are too eclectic and trend-flat. Poivre owns the disciplined, edited, quiet-luxury version of this corner.

### Direct Competitors

Three brands are operating in or adjacent to our space and deserve close monitoring:

**LoveEcru**
- Premium home textiles and tabletop, neutral palette, slow-living narrative.
- *Strength:* editorial photography, strong aesthetic discipline.
- *Gap we exploit:* narrow product range, higher price points, less sculptural/object-led.

**Deco Signature**
- Curated home objects with a design-store sensibility.
- *Strength:* curation, point of view.
- *Gap we exploit:* less consistent visual identity, weaker drop-based storytelling.

**The Oblist**
- Marketplace model for design-led home goods.
- *Strength:* breadth and discovery.
- *Gap we exploit:* marketplace ≠ brand. They are a destination; we are an identity.

### Our Right to Win

We win because we are the only brand sitting at the four-way intersection of:

- **Quiet-luxury aesthetic** — restraint, materiality, no loud logos.
- **Sculptural / artistic design** — objects that read as small works, not commodities.
- **Accessible pricing** — priced for the 25-year-old in a one-bedroom, not the 45-year-old in a townhouse.
- **Trend-aware digital branding** — a brand built for TikTok, Reels, and Pinterest first.

Any one of these is common. Two together exists. Three is rare. All four — that is the Poivre lane.

## 04 · Target Customer

Our customer is a woman aged 22–40 who treats her home as an extension of her identity. She is design-literate even if she has never opened a design book — she has been trained by Pinterest, TikTok, and Instagram to recognize the difference between "fine" and "considered." She wants her home to feel calm, expensive, and personally hers, and she shops by feel rather than category.

We segment her into two primary cohorts:

### Cohort A — Emma (Gen Z, 22–28)

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Age | 22–28 (born ~1998–2003) |
| Occupation | Marketing, creative, communications, early-career professional |
| Income | Entry-level salary, but disproportionately high spend on aesthetics, lifestyle, and home |
| Living situation | Small apartment in a major city, often shared with roommates or partner |
| Interests | Home décor, Pinterest, skincare and beauty, café culture, candles, hosting friends, kitchenware as styling |
| Buying trigger | Feeds. She sees an object styled in a creator's apartment and wants the same feeling. |
| What she wants | Stylish tableware and small objects she can display, photograph, and rearrange. A home that feels elevated for her stage of life. |
| Price sensitivity | High in absolute terms, low relative to perceived aesthetic value — she will spend €40 on a pepper mill if it photographs well. |

### Cohort B — Sophie (Millennial, 30–40)

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Age | 30–40 (born ~1986–1996) |
| Occupation | Project manager, architect, designer, consultant, founder |
| Income | Stable, upper-middle-class |
| Living situation | Well-designed apartment or first home, often with a partner |
| Interests | Interior design, cooking, travel, architecture, wellness, slow living, quality over quantity |
| Buying trigger | Considered. She researches, saves, and buys deliberately. Trust and material quality matter. |
| What she wants | A calm, beautiful home. Pieces that feel premium without being flashy or trend-coded. Objects she will keep for a decade. |
| Price sensitivity | Will spend more, but expects justification through material, design, and brand integrity. |

### What They Share

- Both shop visually-first, on social platforms before websites.
- Both reject loud luxury logos.
- Both treat their home as a stage for hosting, self-care, and identity.
- Both want to be seen as having taste — not as having spent money.

### What Separates Them

- Emma is buying/renting her first "real" home objects and learning her taste. We meet her with entry-priced statement pieces.
- Sophie is editing and elevating her home. We meet her with mid-range investment pieces and limited collections.

### Customer Insight (The "Why")

> *"I want my home to feel like I belong in it — not like I bought it from a store."*

That sentence is the brief for every product, photograph, and email Poivre produces.

## 05 · Brand Foundations

### Purpose

To make quiet-luxury living accessible — so that beauty, ritual, and restraint are not reserved for the few.

### Vision

A generation whose homes look and feel considered, regardless of budget. A world where the everyday object — the pepper mill, the carafe, the soap dish — is treated with the same care as fine art.

### Mission

To design, curate, and release small collections of home and tableware objects that combine sculptural form, natural materials, and accessible pricing — sold directly to the customer through a brand built for the social-first era.

### Brand Values

Four words anchor every decision. They were not chosen as marketing copy — they are operating filters.

**Curated**
We release small, edited collections. We say no more than we say yes. Every piece earns its place. We are not a catalog; we are a point of view.

**Timeless**
We design for the decade, not the season. Our objects should age into a home, not out of it. Trend-aware in marketing, trend-resistant in product.

**Refined**
Restraint is the discipline. Forms are simple but considered. Finishes are honest. Nothing is decorative for its own sake.

**Raw Aesthetic**
We let natural materials speak. Stone shows its veining, wood its grain, ceramic its hand. Refinement without sterility — luxury that feels lived-in.

### Brand Essence

**Refined rawness, made everyday.**

## 06 · Brand Personality & Voice

### Personality Archetype

Poivre is the **Aesthete** — a creator archetype with the discipline of the connoisseur. She is the well-traveled friend with the apartment everyone wants to photograph: warm but not effusive, confident but not loud, generous with taste but never preachy.

### Voice Attributes

- **Considered, not chatty.** We write the way we design — with restraint. Short, declarative sentences. Few adverbs.
- **Warm, not casual.** We are personable, but we do not over-share or over-emoji. The brand has poise.
- **Sensorial, not technical.** We describe how an object feels in the hand, the light, the room — not its specs.
- **French in spirit, not in cliché.** Paris is a sensibility — restraint, material honesty, daily ritual. Not berets, baguettes, or "ooh la la."
- **Quietly confident.** We assume the reader has taste. We never explain why something is beautiful.

### Voice in Practice — Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "An object that grinds pepper." | "Our amazing new pepper mill is finally here!!" |
| "Cut from stone. Made for slow mornings." | "This stunning marble tray is perfect for any occasion." |
| "A small collection. Limited by design." | "Shop our huge new collection — over 50 pieces!" |
| "Set the table for yourself first." | "Treat yourself, you deserve it 💕✨" |
| "Drop 03. Linen, and a few other things." | "NEW DROP ALERT 🚨🔥 don't miss out!!" |

### Tone Adjustments by Context

- **Social captions:** short, atmospheric, one or two lines maximum.
- **Product pages:** sensorial and material-led; lead with feel, end with spec.
- **Email:** feels like a note from a friend with taste — never a promotion.
- **Customer service:** warm, prompt, human; the brand voice does not turn corporate when something goes wrong.

## 07 · Visual Identity

### Logo

#### Logo System

The Poivre identity is built on two marks that work as a system: the primary wordmark and the "P" monogram. Both are drawn by the same hand — the same flowing script, the same subtle ink-grain texture in the strokes — so they feel native to one another rather than a logo and a derivative. The system gives us range without diluting the brand: the wordmark carries the name when there is room to breathe, the monogram carries the brand when there is not.

#### Primary Wordmark

A custom hand-drawn script reading "Poivre," paired with a tracked-out sans-serif "PARIS" set beneath the body of the word. The script is the brand — warmth, hand, craft, a touch of the Parisian café — while the small sans-serif anchors it in modern restraint and locates us geographically. The subtle ink texture in the strokes is intentional: it reads as drawn, not generated. Refinement that still has a hand in it. This is the brand essence — refined rawness — compressed into a single mark.

Use the primary wordmark wherever there is room to give it presence:

- Website header, About page, hero moments
- Product pages, lookbooks, editorial layouts
- Packaging exteriors, hangtags, dust bags
- Printed cards, letterheads, invoices
- Email headers and footers
- Storefront signage and event collateral

#### Monogram — The "P"

The "P" lifted from the wordmark, with the same descending loop and grain. The monogram is for tight spaces, repeating patterns, and moments where the brand needs to be felt without being announced. It is the brand at its most confident — a single letter doing the work of a name.

Use the monogram wherever the wordmark would feel oversized, redundant, or visually noisy:

- Social media avatars, favicons, app icons
- Embossed or debossed details — wax seals, tissue paper, soap bars, ceramic underside marks
- Repeating patterns — gift wrap, fabric, tote interiors, packing tape
- Product tags and care labels where space is tight
- Watermarks on photography or editorial PDFs
- Loyalty and gifting touchpoints — thank-you cards, stickers, member-only marks

#### When to Use Which

| Use the wordmark when… | Use the monogram when… |
|---|---|
| The viewer does not yet know the brand | The viewer already knows the brand |
| There is horizontal space to breathe | Space is square, tight, or repeating |
| The mark is the primary subject | The mark is a signature on something else |
| Formality matters — invoices, packaging, signage | Intimacy matters — seals, tags, finishing details |
| Print is large — books, signage, full-bleed editorial | Print is small — buttons, hardware, embroidery |

#### Clear Space

Both marks need air. As a working rule, leave clear space around the mark equal to the height of the "P" in the wordmark (or the height of the monogram itself). No type, photography edge, or graphic element should encroach into that zone. The marks are designed with their own visual margin built in — respect it.

#### Minimum Size

- **Wordmark:** minimum 24 mm / 90 px wide in print, 120 px wide on screen. Below this, the "PARIS" line becomes unreadable and the grain texture muddies. Use the monogram instead.
- **Monogram:** minimum 12 mm / 40 px on screen. Below this, the descending loop loses definition. For favicons or app icons below this size, a simplified single-weight version may be commissioned.

#### Color Application

- **Primary:** soft black (~`#1A1A1A`) on cream (~`#F5EDE2`). The default, and the highest-fidelity expression of the brand.
- **Reversed:** cream on soft black, or cream on cocoa (~`#6B4423`). Used for packaging, dark editorial moments, and night-mode digital surfaces.
- **Tonal:** cocoa on cream, or bordeaux on cream, used sparingly for capsule releases, gifting, or seasonal stationery.
- **Embossed / debossed / foiled:** preferred on tactile surfaces — paper, leather, soap, ceramic. The mark reads even when it carries no ink at all. This is the brand at its most quiet, and its most luxurious.
- **Never:** on photography without a treated background, on a noisy gradient, or in any color outside the brand palette.

#### What Not to Do

- Do not redraw, re-trace, re-space, or re-set the marks. The hand-drawn quality and the texture are the brand.
- Do not stretch, condense, skew, or rotate. The marks are designed to sit level.
- Do not add drop shadows, outer glows, bevels, or any digital effect.
- Do not place the marks on busy or low-contrast backgrounds. If the photograph is loud, the mark goes on a clean field next to it.
- Do not separate "Poivre" from "PARIS" in the wordmark. They are a single locked unit.
- Do not pair the wordmark and the monogram together in the same composition — they are alternates, not a lockup.
- Do not use the monogram in body copy as a substitute for the word "Poivre." It is a mark, not a letter.

### Typography

- **Brand logotype:** custom script (Poivre wordmark).
- **Editorial display:** a contrasted high-low serif (e.g. a Didone or modern serif) for headlines and editorial layouts.
- **Body:** a clean, classic sans (e.g. a humanist sans) for ecommerce and email.
- **Captions / labels:** tracked-out small caps for product names and section labels.

### Photography Direction

- **Light:** natural, directional, soft. Long shadows are part of the brand.
- **Surfaces:** stone, marble, linen, kraft, plaster, raw wood. No glossy seamless.
- **Color story:** cream, cocoa, bordeaux, stone — restrained. Pops of fruit (cherries, grapefruit, fig) for warmth.
- **Composition:** still-life-led. Objects in use, not floating on white. A coffee mid-sip. An apron mid-day.
- **Texture:** always visible. Veining, grain, hand-marks, paper folds — the rawness in refined rawness.
- **What we never do:** overlit packshots, stock interiors, lifestyle clichés, AI-glossy renders.

### Packaging & Unboxing

Packaging is the first physical brand encounter and is treated as a product in itself.

- Kraft and cream outer boxes — honest, recyclable, materially aligned.
- Cream tissue or vellum wrap with the script logo, single color.
- A printed thank-you card in bordeaux script — warm, hand-signed in spirit.
- No plastic, no excess.

## 08 · Product Strategy

### Operating Model — Drops, Not Catalog

Poivre releases curated drops rather than maintaining a permanent catalog. Each drop is a tightly edited collection built around a single material story, season, or ritual. Drops create urgency, protect aesthetic discipline, and give the brand a publishing cadence rather than a retail one.

### Core Product Pillars

- **Tabletop.** Pepper mills, salt cellars, carafes, glassware, serving boards, trays. The category that started the brand.
- **Kitchen objects.** Olive oil bottles, utensil holders, ceramic vessels — pieces that live on the counter and earn display space.
- **Hosting & ritual.** Candles, soaps, table linens, small entertaining objects.
- **Home accents.** Vases, bookends, small sculptural objects — the "art for the surface."

### Product Principles

- Every object must work as both a tool and a piece of décor.
- Material honesty: stone is stone, wood is wood, ceramic is ceramic.
- Sculptural silhouette over surface decoration.
- Priced to be bought without permission — accessible enough for the impulse, considered enough for the keepsake.
- Designed to be photographed but built to be used.

### Price Architecture (Working Bands)

| Tier | Range & role |
|---|---|
| Entry / impulse | €20–€50 — soaps, candles, small ceramics. Gateway to the brand. |
| Core / signature | €50–€150 — pepper mills, carafes, trays. The hero products. |
| Investment | €150–€400 — limited materials, marble, larger pieces. The brag-worthy hero. |
| Capsule / limited | €400+ — collaborations, numbered editions, one-of-one. |

## 09 · Go-to-Market & Funnel

Our funnel is structured around five stages: Awareness, Consideration, Trial/Conversion, Loyalty, and Advocacy. Each stage has its own job, content type, and platform.

### Awareness — Stop the Scroll

*Job: make someone feel something in three seconds.*

- **Content:** cinematic apartment clips, morning and evening rituals, table styling, hosting moments, ambient lighting, slow-living visuals, "objects that make your home feel expensive."
- **Visual style:** close textures, soft shadows, tactile materials, movement and light reflection, minimal text.
- **Platforms:** TikTok, Instagram Reels, Pinterest.
- **KPIs:** reach, save rate, share rate, hook-rate at 3 seconds.

### Consideration — From "Pretty" to "Worth Buying"

*Job: turn aesthetic affinity into intent.*

- **Content:** product styling and function — how objects are used, scale, material close-ups; UGC and creator reviews; founder and brand storytelling; design philosophy; inspiration moodboards.
- **Mechanics:** email capture with 10% off, early-access drops, styling guides, mini-stories.
- **KPIs:** email signups, return visits, time on product pages, save-to-buy conversion.

### Trial / Conversion — Reassurance, Urgency, Trust

*Job: close the gap between want and purchase.*

- **Product pages:** editorial-grade photography, lifestyle photos in context, sensorial copy.
- **Trust signals:** customer reviews with photos, transparent shipping, clear returns, "seen in" content.
- **Retargeting:** Instagram and TikTok retargeting on viewed products and abandoned carts.
- **Bundles:** hosting set, bedside bundle, kitchen styling set, candle and tray combo — composed for the moment, not the cart.
- **KPIs:** conversion rate, AOV, return-customer rate, refund/return rate.

### Loyalty & Advocacy — Become a Brand, Not a Store

*Job: turn buyers into a community. This is where the business compounds.*

- **Community feeling:** name our customers, share their homes, build small rituals around drops.
- **Newsletter:** an editorial publication, not a promo email — process, inspiration, the people behind a piece, founder's insights/messages.
- **Drops over catalog:** rhythmic releases that customers anticipate. Sold-out is a feature.
- **Referral and gifting:** gift cards, hosting kits, a referral mechanic that fits the brand.
- **KPIs:** repeat purchase rate, organic UGC volume, newsletter open and click rate, drop sell-through.

### Channel Priorities (12 months)

- **Owned:** ecommerce site, email list, SMS. The home base.
- **Earned organic:** TikTok, Instagram, Pinterest. Daily publishing, weekly priority content.
- **Paid:** Meta and TikTok ads, used for retargeting and top-of-funnel scaling of winning organic content.
- **Partnerships:** creator seeding (home, food, hosting), boutique stockists in Paris and one or two key cities, editorial PR.
- **Retail:** pop-ups before permanent retail. Pop-ups are content as much as commerce.

| Category | Premium-feeling, accessibly priced home and tableware |
| Target | Style-aware women, 22–40, building a home that reflects them |

| Reasons to believe | Material honesty, drop-based curation, French sensibility, editorial visual standard |
| Brand essence | Refined rawness, made everyday |
| One-liner | Quiet luxury for the 21st century girl |

### What We Are

- A point of view, not a product range.
- A publishing brand with a tabletop business attached.
- Disciplined. Edited. Quietly confident.
- Paris in sensibility, global in audience.

### What We Are Not

- Not a marketplace, not a catalog.
- Not luxury in the gatekept, logo-loud sense.
- Not French clichés — Paris is a discipline, not a costume.
- Not fast home — every piece is meant to age into a home.

### Decision Filters

Before any product, photograph, partnership, or message is approved, it must answer yes to all five:

- **Curated:** would we still include this if we could only release ten things this year?
- **Timeless:** would this still belong in the brand in five years?
- **Refined:** is anything here decorative for its own sake? If yes, remove it.
- **Raw:** does the material honesty come through? Can you feel it?
- **Accessible:** could our 25-year-old customer buy this without asking permission?
